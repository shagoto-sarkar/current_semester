RESULTS VS. DISCUSSION VS. CONCLUSION
 In

the results section, the data are presented
simply and objectively. No speculation or
interpretation is given.
 In the discussion section, the meaning of the
results is interpreted and put into context (e.g.,
compared with other findings in the literature),
and its importance is assigned.
 In the conclusion section, the results and the
main conclusion are summarized and ensure that
objectives are fulfilled.

DISCUSSION

DISCUSSION IN BRIEF
 Summary: A brief recap of your key results
 Interpretations: What do your results mean?
 Implications: Why do your results matter?
 Limitations: What can’t your results tell us?
 Recommendations: Avenues for further studies or

analyses

STEPS
 Step 1: Restate your research problem and research

questions
 Step 2: Summarize your key findings
The data suggest that…
The data support/oppose the theory that…
The analysis identifies…
Step 3: Interpret your results
How do your results relate with those of previous
studies?
If you get results that differ from those of previous
studies, why may this be the case?
What do your results contribute to your field of
research?
What other explanations could there be for your
findings?

Step 6:Deliberate the implications

What new contributions do they add to the existing knowledge?

TO AVOID
 Don’t

introduce new results: You should only
discuss the data that you have already reported
in your results section.
 Don’t
make inflated claims: Avoid over
interpretation and speculation that isn’t directly
supported by your data.
 Don’t undermine your research: The discussion of
limitations should aim to strengthen your
credibility, not emphasize weaknesses or failures.
 Tables and figures (these are usually included in
the results section)
